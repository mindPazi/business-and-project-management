  \item ISO/IEC 27001:2022 --- Annex A.8.2, A.8.11, A.8.15
\end{itemize}
\textbf{To defend at the oral:} state \emph{why} each standard is cited (what gap it closes) and which pillar it maps to. The mapping table in \S1.3 of \texttt{src.tex} is the revision aid.

\textbf{To study:}

\section{Part I --- The Four Pillars}

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{\S2 Governance (Pillar I) \ \statusdone}
\concetto{Stakeholder management and responsibility assignment. The five governance roles are the project's stakeholders, the Board is the governing body, and the RACI matrix assigns accountability. Engagement strategy follows power/interest; communication follows push/pull.}
\textbf{To study:}
\begin{itemize}
  \slide{65}{Organizational structures: authority, responsibility, accountability}{accountability is not shared and equals authority $+$ responsibility; the principle behind the single-Accountable rule.}
  \slide{70--73}{Responsibility matrix / RACI}{linear responsibility chart (LRC / RAM); R / A / C / I, with exactly one Accountable per task (p.~72) and a worked example (p.~73). Underpins the RACI table in \S2.4.}
  \slide{75--76}{Push vs Pull communication}{push $=$ actively sent (policy changes, incidents); pull $=$ retrieved on demand (approval records in the audit log).}
  \slide{201--206}{Stakeholder management / Power/Interest grid}{classify by power and interest: manage closely, keep satisfied, keep informed, or monitor. Used to place DPO, Legal, IT Security, document owners and senior management.}
\end{itemize}

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{\S3 Human-in-the-Loop Controls (Pillar II) \ \statusdone}
\concetto{Risk-based prioritization and decision-making under uncertainty. Document classification applies a probability/impact logic (sensitivity $\times$ exposure $\times$ audience) to route human attention; escalation is structured decision-making.}
\textbf{To study:}

  \slide{44}{Leadership and soft skills: decision-making}{unilateral versus group-based decision-making, diverge/converge, and the escalation mechanism; the basis for the gated escalation in \S3.3.}
  \slide{301--307}{Probability/Impact matrix}{the scoring logic behind the three-tier classification (\S3.1 and \S3.2) and the level of human control each tier triggers.}
\end{itemize}

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{\S4 Audit Logging and Accountability (Pillar III) \ \statusdone}
\concetto{Accountability and documented evidence. This pillar rests on external regulation rather than the course slides: the duty to \emph{demonstrate} compliance, not merely to achieve it.}
\textbf{Note: this section draws mainly on external sources, not course slides.} The references are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item GDPR Art.~5(2) accountability; Art.~5(1)(e) storage limitation; Art.~32 confidentiality.

\concetto{Lessons learned and root-cause analysis. The case is read as a post-incident review: a Five Whys drill traces the breach to a missing governance layer, and every gap turns out to be an accountability or unmanaged-risk failure, not a technical one.}
\textbf{To study:}

\subsection{\S10 Counterfactual Analysis \ \statusdone}
\concetto{Expected Monetary Value on a decision tree. The publication step is modelled as a decision node (control on / off) leading to chance nodes (breach / no breach); EMV $=$ probability $\times$ impact folds the tree back. The flaw of averages explains why the tail, not the mean, justifies dual control.}
\textbf{To study:}
\begin{itemize}
  \slide{301--307}{Probability/Impact matrix}{the probability and impact inputs feeding the EMV (20\% vs 2\%; EUR 500,000 impact).}
  \slide{308--311}{Flaw of averages}{why an EMV mean understates a low-frequency, catastrophic outcome; the Post Office event is that tail.}
  \slide{322--330}{Expected Monetary Value / decision tree}{the core method of \S10: EMV with vs without the framework, net of incremental cost.}
  \slide{351--356}{Monte Carlo / distribution thinking}{reinforces the tail argument behind the very-high gate (dual control rather than a cost-benefit test).}
\end{itemize}
